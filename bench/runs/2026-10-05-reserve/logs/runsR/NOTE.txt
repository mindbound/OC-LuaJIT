- In EVERY multi-refusal run, the probe's paint failures (CAP-X pf) equal the refusals minus one:
  pf 2/n 3, pf 4/n 5, pf 3/n 4, pf 1/n 2 ... (15 of 15).  So every refusal but the last was

  sequence ending outside a handler instead; pf there was the LAST SUCCESSFUL paint's figure.
- WHERE the absorbed ones land: all on the sandbox thread (kernel=0, wd=1), all in the KERNEL's

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE DESIGN.
- The absorber in the machine is sandbox-thread Lua code of the kernel's (invoke wrapper, checkArg)
  under the PROGRAM-ADJACENT pcall (the paint's; in OpenOS it would be the dispatcher's or none).
  The shim cannot tell it from the program except by the nearest Lua frame's chunkname ("=machine"
  vs the program's) -- the map's L8, fragile and a policy choice; not recommended.
- The recorder lever (L1) is irrelevant in the machine (0 of 50).
- The string-table lever (L7) did not fire: no refusal's request equals a table doubling.
- What bounds this is the reserve's SIZE and LIFETIME (L3/L4) or the verdict's DELIVERY: the
  verdict decided at a proof is executed by the first crossing after it, which after a yield is
  the paint's 64 B closure (the kernel's wrapper), not the fill's next record.
- Note the geometry: the first proof after a resume happens at the paint (the first allocations of
  the step), so "deliver the verdict only after the next resume" would land it on the paint still.
  A rule of the kind "a verdict refusal opens no reserve" would break W8 (W8's own first refusal is
  a verdict under THE WINDOW), unless the reserve a verdict opens is small (a few KiB, L3).
