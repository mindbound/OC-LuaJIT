Ranking: 1. R4K  2. THE RECIPIENT  3. THE ADDRESS.

R4K and THE RECIPIENT's rule 3 are the same lever (the reserve a refusal opens reaches 4 KiB past
the heap it was refused at, clamped to [G/2, G]); they differ in robustness details (section 3)
and in what else is bundled.  Both make the row's defect bounded UNCONDITIONALLY: whoever absorbs
the first refusal, the fill gets 4 KiB + the window instead of G/2 + the window (16 KiB at the
floor, 256 KiB at 8 MB), and the stated sandbox bound tightens from total + G + 4 KiB to
max(u, total + G/2) + 8 KiB.  THE ADDRESS leaves the reserve's size alone and tries to deliver the
verdict to the right handler by a frame-walk majority vote; when the vote is right the second
chance vanishes (hermetically it always was right); when it is wrong the design is TODAY'S DEFECT
PLUS 8 KiB (M-A4), and I find two mechanisms that make it wrong in a real machine (M-A1, M-A2).
Under "the player picks the workload" a conditional fix whose failure mode is today-plus cannot be
the base.

1. COMMON GROUND (true of all three, checked against the shipped shim 3186e87)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Nothing allocates inside the allocator; no back-off; no knob; zero LuaJIT lines (THE ADDRESS
  and THE RECIPIENT include lj_frame.h for macros only).  build-native.sh's source gates pass per
  the designers; the collector gate forbids nothing they add.
- HOOK_GC and a host GCSTOP: all three return before any new write (R4K src-plain-R4K:1289-1293
  sits after the HOOK_GC guard; identify src-id:1356-1358; verdict src-v:1403-1405 and the
  existing lend/credit guards).  norefuse: credit is never consulted; the vote returns early.
- C mode and legacy: each design's two lines sit on both paths (the shipped :409-414 and
  :476-485 shapes); the dropin objects pass each suite.
- The fresh-record rule (W11) keeps G in all three (R4K :1204, identify :1247; THE ADDRESS does
  not touch lj52_gc_reserve).
- The kernel's slice: R4K/identify leave the arithmetic (kernel top = tier credit + K, the
  sandbox's ceiling = tier credit + L, margin K - L = 12 KiB in both tiers).  THE ADDRESS adds H =
  8 KiB to the kernel's BURST top (src-v:1294-1295, THE CADENCE :1535) so the margin survives a
  held crossing; the every-thread bound holds because H <= G/2 is checked at compile time
  (src-v:1211-1214).
- W2b/W2c: every refusal still arms from any headroom in all three (identify's absorbed branch
  arms at :1367; THE ADDRESS's R3 keeps the arm at :1437-1438).
- W8: passes on all three.  A finding the lifetime designer made and the other two did not: W8
  runs on the MAIN thread (kernel slice, wd_depth 0) and passes with a reserve of 0, so W8 does
  not guard the reserve's size at all.  Only lifetime's W21 does (section 3.1).
- THE WINDOW's own residuals stay in all three: the reserve-top verdict's landing lottery (fewer
  draws under R4K/identify; served to the addressee under THE ADDRESS when it is right), the
  kernel's 2 KiB per resume overrunning the window at the dispatcher's pack (k2048: 725 of 2 048
  string caps), F1's burst-top landing.  No design claims otherwise.
- In the machine (NOTE.txt): every absorbed refusal was a 56-64 B closure made by machine.lua's
  invoke path (wrapUserdata/unwrapUserdata/checkArg: :1261/:1280/:117) under the probe's
  pcall(paint); the recorder absorbed 0 of 50; the string table 0 of 50.  So the recorder levers
  (identify rule 1, THE ADDRESS R3) are hermetic hygiene in the machine, and the size lever (R4K =
  identify rule 3) and the delivery lever (THE ADDRESS) are the two that can matter there.

2. THE LENS QUESTIONS, ANSWERED FOR EACH DESIGN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Is the bound true in every path?
  R4K:  sandbox used + delta <= max(u, T + G/2) + 4 KiB + 4 KiB, every thread + 16 KiB instead
        of the sandbox's 4 KiB; through lj52_gc_rsvcredit's clamp also <= T + G (+L / +K).  The
        clamp is applied where the credit is READ (:1213-1216, called at :1234, :1342, :1391,
        :2114), so a cap changed after the tier opened re-clamps, and no path can read a credit
        under G/2 or over G whatever gc_rsv holds.  Sized once per episode (:1289), only after
        the HOOK_GC/GCSTOP/norefuse/gc_busy guards; the fresh branch sets G (:1204).  Measured:
        0 of 10 240 runs past either bound (compare-R4K ">old"/">new" columns all 0).
  RECIPIENT: the same inequality via lj52_gc_odfor (:1261-1266), clamped when STORED.  Read raw
        at :1256 and :1301.  True today; see M-I1 for the two ways it can stop being true.
  ADDRESS: unchanged absolute bound.  A HELD crossing may reach T + G/2 + L + H in burst
        (:1322-1323) and the reserve top + L in reserve; H <= G/2 guarded (:1211-1214); the kernel
        keeps K - L past either (W22 shows the 11 KiB table granted with the sandbox 2 KiB past
        its window).  W7 od_peak G + 4 078; W21's Bto reaches the hold ceiling within 400 B.  A
        verdict standing across a refusal: a non-recorder refusal still sets gc_win = 0
        (:1433-1435); a recorder refusal leaves it standing (R3), which is consistent -- live >
        top is still true -- and bounded by the same ceilings.

2.2 Can a program ratchet it (repeated caught refusals)?
  R4K: no.  Resized only when the tier opens (:1289); rsvcreep (resize at every refusal) is
       caught only by the re-scoped W7 -- which is why that re-scope is part of the design, and
       the designer says so.  An episode ends only at a proof under the cap, so catch-hold-catch
       stays under max(u, S) + 8 KiB.
  RECIPIENT: no, the same once-per-episode rule (:1370); R4 pins u3 - u2 <= window + 1 KiB.
  ADDRESS: no.  The hold's ceiling is absolute; two alternating data-keeping handlers flip the
       candidate and the held one meets the ceiling; W7 and W7k pass unchanged.

2.3 A recovery that is not the probe's: an OpenOS catch handler that prints through the tty
    (1-3 KB across several pcalls and a yield) before dropping.
  R4K / RECIPIENT: after the program's OWN refusal at u, the top is max(u, S) + 4 KiB and the
       window lends 4 KiB past that: at least 8 KiB from u minus whatever the window had already
       lent before the verdict (typically a few hundred bytes to ~1 KiB).  The recovery's
       formatting garbage is collected by the cycles the crossings arm; what must stay under
       ~4 KiB is HELD data across proofs.  W21 pins ~2 KB held + ~8 KB granted (R0 and R1K fail
       it; R4K passes).  A yield does not close the tier (L4 was built, measured and rejected:
       39 of 803 stalls, same as shipped).  During the yield other processes' live allocations
       share the same 4 KiB -- stock gives 0, the shipped shim 16-256 KiB.  OpenOS's
       event.onError path (io.open + a traceback write + close) was sized by nobody: the map
       estimates 2-5 KB, mostly transient.  That is the edge of 4 KiB; R8K passes everything
       but the two cases that pin 4 096.  See M-L1.
  ADDRESS: the program's own refusal opens the full G/2 reserve as today; the recovery has
       16-256 KiB.  Generous -- and the reason the design does not bound the second chance.

2.4 What breaks W8 / W2b / W2c / W11, and is each re-scope honest?
  Nothing breaks in any design.  Re-scopes:
  R4K: W19's precondition (reads the tier, 14th WALLSTATS value) -- honest, the property the case
       exists for still holds and is shown passing on both objects; W7 TIGHTENED to a fixed
       bound that the shipped shim fails (36 846 against 24 702) -- honest and load-bearing.
       Housekeeping the implementer must do: `unbounded`'s expected set, W7k reading the tier at
       G > 32 KiB, W16R's topr.
  RECIPIENT: W19 (same); W16R/W16Rj/W16RL/W16RjL read the tier from od_state instead of the
       refusal count -- required by rule 1 (a counted refusal can now open nothing) and honest;
       all four pass on both objects and on the shipped one.
  ADDRESS: C6b re-scoped because the kernel's burst top really moves (+8 KiB) -- honest, and a
       real change to the stated kernel arithmetic; negative-control 4.19 refusalkeeps shrinks to
       W19 (the hold rescues the landing it used to produce); 4.17 rawverdict's expected set
       EMPTIES: no case fails when the two-cycle rule is removed.  The designer calls this
       unacceptable and proposes W26 (not built).  I agree it is unacceptable: under THE ADDRESS
       THE WINDOW's two-cycle rule becomes unobservable by the suite.  See M-A5.

3.1 R4K (lifetime/src-plain-R4K/lj52shim.c; R4K.diff)
  M-L1 MAJOR (the only one): THE 4 KiB FLOOR IS AT THE EDGE OF A REAL RECOVERY.  W21 shows
       ~2 KB held survives and R1K does not; nothing measures OpenOS's onError path or a
       traceback-printing handler in the machine.  A recovery that holds more than ~4 KiB across
       the proofs its own allocations arm is refused inside its report, where the shipped shim
       allowed G/2 and stock allows nothing.  The designer states this ((g)) and offers R8K
       (passes all but W20/W20L and the re-scoped W7, which pin 4 096 by construction).  Fix:
       measure the onError path with the instrumented native before fixing R, or pin 8 192.
  m-L2 minor: a program that catches TWO refusals in one episode and keeps its data gets only the
       window after the second (the once rule).  Identify states it ((g)); lifetime implies it.
       Stock gives such a program nothing.
  m-L3 minor: the reserve-top lottery remains: 6 of 803 second chances over three seeds end
       [paint, dispatcher] at probe2oe:81 (0.7 %, from 5.7 %).  The reduction's mechanism is a
       stated hypothesis (d.3: phase), not a proof; the gate's (3) is where it is judged.  Honest.
  m-L4 minor: the instrumented copy's OCLJREF credit/top/over fields compute the old arithmetic
       (identify notes the same for its copy); the in-machine gate must read the tier value.
  Strengths that matter for safety: two scalar writes and one 4-line helper; the clamp on the
       read side (robust to cap change and to any future RESERVE write); byte-identical driver
       lines on every cap whose program saw its own refusal (10 240 of 10 240); every new check
       seen failing in the direction it guards (R0 fails W21, R1K fails W16Rj/W16RjL/W21, R8K
       fails W20/W20L/W7, rsvwhole/rsvzero/rsvfresh/rsvunclamped/rsvcreep each caught by a named
       case); L4 and L6 rejected by evidence rather than argument.

3.2 THE RECIPIENT (identify/src-id/lj52shim.c; identify.diff)
  M-I1 MAJOR (robustness, not reachable today): THE RESERVE CREDIT IS CLAMPED WHEN STORED AND
       READ RAW.  gc_odcredit is set at :1247 (fresh: G) and :1370 (odfor, clamped to [G/2, G]
       at that moment) and read unclamped at :1256 and :1301.  (a) A cap lowered while the record
       is in RESERVE leaves a credit above the new G: the stated bound total + G + 4 KiB is then
       false by (old credit - new G).  OC does not lower a running machine's cap except around
       persist, where GCSTOP parks everything, so today this needs the test API; (b) any future
       write of gc_odstate = RESERVE that forgets gc_odcredit yields credit 0: the reserve top AT
       the cap, i.e. a sandbox refused harder in RESERVE than in BURST.  There are exactly two
       such writes in the shipped shim (:1183, :1259) and the diff covers both; the trap is for
       the next editor.  R4K's lj52_gc_rsvcredit prevents both by construction.  Fix: read
       through a clamp, as R4K does -- one helper.
  M-I2 MAJOR (evidence): THE STALL CLAIM IS ONE SEED.  "21 -> 0" in the OpenOS-shaped string
       cell is seed 1 only; the same rule under lifetime's three-seed check reads 6 of 803.  The
       document claims only what it ran, but a reader will take 0 for the rule's rate.  The
       4 KiB floor against a HOLDING recovery is not tested here either (R4 pins the size and
       the once rule; there is no W21-like case), so identify's R1b claim rests on reasoning.
  Rule 1 (the recorder's refusal opens no reserve, shuts no window, consumes no verdict;
       lj52_gc_absorbed :1270-1283, used at :1363-1369): CORRECT and proven both ways.  The
       predicate -- J->state not idle, vmstate not a trace/C/GC/EXIT, the running thread's
       innermost C frame a cpcall frame (nres < 0 and saved PC == L: vm_x64.dasc vm_cpcall) -- is
       the recorder's because trace_state is the only cpcall body that allocates with vmstate
       RECORD/OPT/ASM and runs no Lua under it (so SAVE_PC is never overwritten by BC_FUNCC); a C
       function called by the interpreter during recording runs with vmstate C and is excluded.
       Shown to fire: ctl_rec2 7 of 7 against the --wrap=lj_vm_cpcall ground truth.  Shown NOT to
       fire on a program allocation during recording: dbg_r1b2.lua (a 256 KiB array doubling,
       J->state RECORD, vmstate RECORD, absorbed = 0) and the forensics' 1 092 program-visible
       refusals with J->state RECORD, all read 0 by the identical expression and all caught by
       the program's pcall (F6/F7: (0,1) disagreements 0).  The LITERAL predicate's sabotage
       stalls W16Rj/W16RjL, so the suite can tell the two apart.  A recorder refusal leaving the
       verdict standing is consistent: the next program crossing is refused for data that does
       not fit.  Residual (stated): the recorder's abort still penalises its loop (map L1b).  In
       the machine the recorder opened nothing (0 of 50), so rule 1 is hygiene there plus the
       kernel thread, where it is the kernel's only non-fatal absorber.
  Rule 2 (HOOK_VMEVENT like HOOK_GC: :1299, :1334, :1358): correct.  hook_vmevent/hook_restore
       set and clear the bit only around lj_vmevent_call's lj_vm_pcall, so nothing but the
       handler's own allocations are affected; harness only (no `jit` in the sandbox).  A refusal
       under it does not arm (:1358 returns before the arm) -- the HOOK_GC asymmetry, harmless.
  The S1 recogniser (:1360-1362): a count; the arithmetic 2 (mask + 1) sizeof(GCRef) matches
       lj_str_resize's growth; no behaviour.  Fine.
  Strengths: rule 3 is R4K (sub-S refusals get max(G/2, u + 4 KiB - T) rather than R4K's G/2 +
       4 KiB; both leave >= 8 KiB from u for the recovery); the three levers were measured
       separately (rec / res / id) and the control variant (counters only) is byte-identical to
       the baseline on 3 072 caps; four WALLSTATS values the in-machine gate can read.

3.3 THE ADDRESS (verdict/src-v/lj52shim.c; verdict-shim.diff)
  No FATAL: the absolute bound holds, the kernel's margin holds, no crash is reproduced, and the
  hermetic record is the best of the three (every refusal in every cell caught by the batch;
  14 471 of 14 471 quiet caps identical; the deterministic stall and the 21 OpenOS-shaped stalls
  gone; k2048 string 828 -> 776).  The findings are about where that record cannot transfer.

  M-A1 MAJOR: THE VOTE HAS NO DECAY; THE HANDLER OF RECORD IS THE MAJORITY SINCE THE LAST PROOF
       UNDER THE CAP.  gc_handn is reset only at `if (used <= total) M->gc_handn = 0;` (:1481),
       and every non-matching crossing decrements it by one (:1415).  A machine running near its
       cap -- the only regime where any of this matters -- rarely proves under the cap.  After
       one large episode the candidate holds thousands of votes; a later filler (a new process,
       another thread) is a non-addressee for thousands of crossings, but its hold room (12 KiB
       burst, 4 KiB reserve: :1322) is spent in ~100-250 small crossings.  It meets the hold's
       CEILING before it can become the addressee: refused in whatever code made that crossing,
       the reserve opened at the full G/2 (:1433-1436), today's lottery resumed 8 KiB later.
       The named limit ("a quiet producer behind a chatty consumer") understates this: the
       addressee can be a handler that no longer exists, on a thread that has died (and a freed
       lua_State or GCproto reused at the same address inherits the stale majority, so "at
       worst one verdict served as today" ((g)) is not right either).  Fix would be a windowed or
       per-episode count (reset at a refusal, or at the tier closing) -- a different design.
  M-A2 MAJOR: THE IDENTITY DEPENDS ON EXECUTION MODE.  recff_pcall records pcall as an inlined
       frame (lj_ffrecord.c:528-539 -> lj_record_call).  In LJ_FR2 the inlined frame's link is a
       KNUM in the snapshot (lj_snap.c:77-80), written to the stack only at an exit
       (snap_restoreval) or a tail link (lj_asm_x86.h:2802-2816); slot 1, the entry frame's own
       link, is SNAP_NORESTORE (lj_snap.c:73-74).  So while a trace runs, the walk from jit_base
       (:1367-1369) cannot see a pcall the trace inlined.  A loop `for ... do pcall(step) end`
       compiled as one trace attributes step's allocations to whatever pcall sits BELOW the loop
       (or to NULL) while the interpreter attributes them to pcall(step): one logical handler,
       two identities, and the vote oscillates between them.  The hermetic JIT-on cells pass
       because the probe's loop is INSIDE the pcall'd closure, so its trace starts inside it and
       the pcall frame is below jit_base.  OpenOS's dispatcher is the opposite shape
       (event.lua:62-78: `for id,handler in pairs(copy) ... pcall(handler.callback, ...)`).  Not
       unsafe -- a wrong address is held, then refused at the ceiling -- but it is the design's
       entire gain that depends on it, and the designer did not consider it.
  M-A3 MAJOR (verification): A FRAME WALK IN THE ALLOCATOR ON EVERY GROWTH PAST THE CAP
       (:1364-1389), ON AN INVARIANT LUAJIT DOES NOT ITSELF EXERCISE ON THIS PLATFORM.  I looked
       for a bad read and found none: in the interpreter and in C, L->base is synced before any
       allocation (lj_err_mem's own walk needs it); on a trace [jit_base - 1] is the entry frame's
       interpreter-written link, or the lower frame's after asm_retf (lj_asm_x86.h:790-802), or a
       link asm_stack_restore just stored at a tail link (lj_asm.c:2167); at an exit
       vm_exit_handler sets L->base from jit_base before lj_trace_exit (vm_x64.dasc:2480-2489)
       and lj_snap_restore never rewrites slot 1, so lj_state_growstack (lj_snap.c:966) and
       snap_unsink (:989) allocate over a valid chain; vmstate EXIT excludes the recorder flag;
       frames below base are never rewritten by a trace.  BUT the designer's warrant -- "as
       lj_err_mem reads it", "the refusal path already relies on it" -- is not a proof: on
       Windows x64 LJ_UNWIND_JIT is 1 (lj_arch.h:723-724) and an on-trace OOM unwinds to the exit
       stub FIRST (lj_err.c:379-382), restoring the snapshot before any Lua frame is walked; and
       an OOM inside the exit's restore is caught by trace_exit_cp's cpcall (lj_trace.c:835-842,
       nres = -2 LUAI_MAXSTACK) without a walk.  So the walk rests on invariants LuaJIT keeps
       for other reasons, and the suite exercises only probe shapes: no vararg frame, no
       continuation (metamethod) frame, no coroutine.wrap, no xpcall with a handler, no recursion
       past 64 links (then "addressed": :1383), no trace-inlined pcall.  PLAUSIBLE-safe, unproven.
       If this design were taken, a frame-shape census over an OpenOS boot and shell session
       (the forensics' instrument already carries the walk) must precede it.  The cost itself is
       not a concern (1-4 links as a rule; 64 at most; a few loads against a GC step).
  M-A4 MAJOR: WHEN THE ADDRESS IS WRONG THE DESIGN IS TODAY PLUS 8 KiB.  A non-addressee's
       growth is lent to T + G/2 + 12 KiB in burst, then its ceiling refusal opens the FULL G/2
       reserve (:1433-1436): the second chance is not bounded, it is removed only when the vote
       is right.  R4K and THE RECIPIENT are better than today in every case; THE ADDRESS is
       better in the case it predicts and worse in the cases M-A1/M-A2 produce.  And in the
       machine's own amplified cell the vote may simply be wrong: NOTE.txt's absorbers are
       machine.lua's invoke closures (several 56-64 B closures and two table.packs per gpu.set,
       five gpu.set per paint, all under pcall(paint)) against ten records per batch; the
       designer's estimate (fill ~100, paint ~45) is unsupported by in-machine data and (f)
       concedes that if the paint out-allocates the fill, "(b) will not be met".  That is the
       gate's own configuration.
  M-A5 MAJOR (observability): THE TWO-CYCLE RULE BECOMES UNTESTABLE.  rawverdict's expected set
       empties (a one-proof verdict's harm was its landing on the stage string or the timer
       record, which the hold now rescues); refusalkeeps shrinks to W19.  A regression in THE
       WINDOW's proof would pass the suite.  The designer flags this as unacceptable and proposes
       W26 (estimates only, not built).  Until it exists, the design ships with a known blind
       spot in exactly the mechanism it builds on.
  m-A6 minor: a standing verdict (:1486) formed on garbage pinned across two cycles stands until
       a proof under the top; acknowledged ((g): "better, not right").  R1 PARTLY.
  m-A7 minor: in the RESERVE tier the hold's room is the window only (:1322): in the machine a
       ~3 KB paint at a reserve-top verdict overruns it (holdfail), and the reserve-top lottery
       returns for a program that caught its own first refusal and kept filling (W16R's shape).
       Stated ((g)).
  m-A8 minor: C6b loosened (the kernel's burst top +8 KiB); six more WALLSTATS values; the
       largest diff (+205/-11, ~98 code lines) and a new concept in the allocator.
  Strengths: W20-W25 are fail-first and sharp (the shipped shim fails W20/W21/W22/W25 with a
       dead sandbox); the recorder rule is exact; three revisions each forced by a measurement
       that the first six cells could not show, reported honestly; the kernel-garbage cell
       improves as a side effect (held packs).

5. THE ADDRESS, THE SPECIFIC QUESTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Cost per slow-path call: a vote on every growth past the cap (:409-412, :476-479 hooks): one
    cframe read, 1-4 frame links as a rule, 64 at most; negligible against the lj_gc_step the
    same call may pay.  Not a safety concern.
  Coroutine stacks: the walk is on cur_L's stack, bounds-checked against L->stack/stacksize
    (:1370); a dead thread's address reused is a stale identity (see M-A1).
  Mid-unwind: lj_err_mem allocates nothing after the throw begins; ERRMEM runs no xpcall
    handler (lj_err_throw directly), so no allocation walks a half-unwound stack.
  Trace exit: L->base = jit_base, jit_base = 0 before lj_trace_exit; slot 1 is never rewritten
    by the restore; unsink's allocations walk the entry chain.  Sound (M-A3 for the caveat).
  No Lua function below: base <= bot + 1 returns 0 -> "addressed" (today's behaviour); a C-API
    allocation from Java on the main thread is the kernel anyway.
  cframe chains across resume: only the innermost C frame is read, for the recorder predicate;
    the Lua frame walk never crosses a resume (frames are per thread).  Correct.
  Vote failure modes: two interleaved fills -- the candidate flips, the other is held to the
    ceiling, refused there, the reserve opens (bounded, but G/2); allocations mostly in library
    code under the program's own pcall -- fine, one identity; the dispatcher owning the growths
    (a listener accumulating data) -- the verdict lands in pcall(callback), event.onError logs
    it, the program never learns, as today; a stale majority (M-A1) and a mode-dependent identity
    (M-A2) are the two the designer did not name.
  What a WRONG address does: the verdict reaches a bystander's handler; the program is refused
    up to 12 KiB later at the hold's ceiling, in whatever code made that crossing, and the
    reserve then opens at G/2 as today.  The verdict reaches the program's handler only if the
    program becomes the candidate before the ceiling, which M-A1 shows need not happen.

6. R4K / THE RECIPIENT, THE SPECIFIC QUESTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The 4 KiB floor against a recovery that holds data: section 2.3 and M-L1.  The suite found the
    floor (R1K fails W16Rj with the JIT on: the recorder's allocations share the reserve) and the
    margin above it is measured only at 4 KiB x 96 caps x 2.  R8K is the conservative choice;
    the cost is +70 string objects of creep per second chance.
  The clamp's interaction with the kernel slice: kernel reserve top = credit + K; sandbox
    ceiling = credit + L; margin K - L = 12 KiB in both tiers (W19 re-scoped passes).  The
    kernel's OWN refusal at S + K sizes a credit of G/2 + 20 KiB -- clamped to G at the floor,
    under G from G >= 48 KiB (1 MB caps up) -- so after its own refusal the kernel has +20 KiB
    rather than +G/2; moot, since a kernel refusal is pcall(main) and the machine is down
    whatever the tier (K1/J3/J4).  When the kernel's slice took the heap past the sandbox's
    ceiling before the sandbox was refused, u is what the kernel left and the clamp at G binds
    (observed max credit 24 704 = G/2 + 8 320): the absolute bound is the one that holds there.
    Both documents state this.
  identify's cframe predicate shown to fail on a program allocation during recording: YES --
    dbg_r1b2.lua (absorbed = 0 on a 256 KiB doubling with J->state RECORD), the forensics'
    1 092 program-visible RECORD-state refusals (0 by the identical expression, every one caught
    by the program's pcall per the cpcall-wrap ground truth), and the literal sabotage's two
    stalls.  The one shape not arranged (a program refusal raised while J->state is RECORD, at
    the wall) cannot be, for the reason given: the recorder's own IR growth is refused first.

7. RECOMMENDATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Build the size rule as R4K writes it (the read-side clamp, lj52_gc_rsvcredit), which is THE
  RECIPIENT's rule 3 made robust.  Graft from THE RECIPIENT: rule 1 (exact, proven both ways;
  the kernel thread's only non-fatal absorber; it also ends w16r_rec's "refusal count = tier"
  fiction), rule 2 (free), and the four WALLSTATS values for the in-machine instrument.  Take
  lifetime's W20/W20L/W21 and the re-scoped W7 (the only cases that guard the reserve's SIZE --
  W8 does not), identify's R1/R2/R4 and its `literal` sabotage, and THE ADDRESS's two-handler
  sandbox shapes (W20/W24/W25 bodies) as cross-tests against the merged object.  Decide R after
  measuring OpenOS's event.onError path in the machine with the instrumented native; if it is
  not measured, pin 8 192 and re-pin W20/W7 to it.  Do not build the vote: its failure modes
  (M-A1, M-A2) are reachable by ordinary OpenOS programs, its worst case is today plus 8 KiB
  (M-A4), it puts an unverified frame walk in the allocator (M-A3), and it blinds the suite to
  THE WINDOW's proof (M-A5).  Keep the reserve-top lottery (6 of 803) and the k2048 overrun as
  open rows; neither design claims them.
