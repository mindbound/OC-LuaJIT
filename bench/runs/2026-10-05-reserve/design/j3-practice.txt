RANK FOR SHIPPING NOW: 1. R4K   2. THE RECIPIENT   3. THE ADDRESS.
The reason is not that THE ADDRESS is wrong.  It is that R4K is a 25-line change to a
tier's size that the player cannot observe except as 200 fewer objects in the runs that had
a second chance, and THE ADDRESS is a new mechanism in lj52_gc_lend whose value in the
machine rests on one unmeasured number -- the allocation count per handler per step -- that
the instrumented native can settle this week for free.  Ship the size now; settle the count;
build the hold only if the count says the fill wins.

1. THE QUESTION THE LENS ASKS: HOW OFTEN DOES THE SECOND CHANCE STILL END OUTSIDE A
   HANDLER IN THE MACHINE?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  THE IN-MACHINE BASE RATE, AND WHAT THE NOTE CHANGED ABOUT IT.
The pooled history for the amplified JIT-on record cell (results-wall-window, W + V) is:
21 of 30 runs took the second-chance path (a paint-absorbed first refusal), and 3 of those
21 went down.  So P(bad | second chance) = 3/21 = 14 %, and P(bad | no second chance) = 0
of 9 (and 0 of 1 820 hermetically, F4).  That ratio is the whole row.

The NOTE adds the piece the designers' hermetic probes do not have.  In the machine:
  - every absorbed refusal is a 56-64 B CLOSURE the KERNEL's machine.lua makes on the
    sandbox thread inside a component call (wrapUserdata's wrapRecursively at machine:1261,
    unwrapUserdata's at :1280, checkArg's `check` at :117), caught by the probe's
    pcall(paint).  All 50 refusals: kernel=0, wd=1, vm=INTERP, jst=IDLE, recorder=0.
  - at the RESERVE top the verdict lands on those same closures again, each absorption
    shutting the window and letting the next crossing be lent: the fill gets a 2nd, 3rd and
    4th chance (1-5 refusals per run; the D cell read 3 1 2 5 3 2 3 4 3 4).
  - the recorder absorbed NOTHING (0 of 50); the string table absorbed nothing (0 of 50).
This is a different absorber from the hermetic one.  Hermetically the paint's absorbed
refusals are at probe2.lua:45/:46 -- string.format and a concat in the probe's OWN Lua
(F4: 1 382 + 905 of 2 303).  In the machine they are the kernel's closures inside a
component call.  Nothing in any of the three censuses models that.  So every hermetic
stall count below measures the probe's phase arithmetic, not the machine's absorber.  R4K
says this itself (d.3: "the machine's paint is 3 KB, its steps are compiled differently,
and the probe's phase arithmetic is not the machine's").  identify's headline does not.

1.2  R4K AND THE RECIPIENT: THE SAME ARITHMETIC AT THE POINT THAT MATTERS.
A reserve-opening refusal happens at the window's ceiling, used ~ total + G/2 + 4 KiB.
  R4K:       gc_rsv = max(used - total, G/2) + 4 KiB           = G/2 + 8 KiB
  RECIPIENT: clamp(used + 4 KiB - total, G/2, G)               = G/2 + 8 KiB
Identical.  The censuses confirm it to the byte: R4K's d.1 and identify's "res" variant row
are the same numbers in all seven shared cells (oe string 21 -> 0, k2048 string 828 -> 785
with sc_out 103 -> 60, odx +1432 -> -11548, held ratio 0.9953/0.9954).  So their in-machine
expectation is the same, and everything that separates them is cost, tests and documents
(sections 3-5).  They differ only for a refusal taken BELOW the burst top: R4K then gives
G/2 + 4 KiB, the RECIPIENT gives exactly G/2.  That costs the RECIPIENT one real shape --
see 4.3.

1.3  MY OWN EXTRAPOLATION FOR R4K / THE RECIPIENT, WITH ITS ARITHMETIC.
The reserve's size does not change WHERE a verdict lands.  It changes (a) the phase of the
reserve top inside the batch cycle -- R4K's d.3 hypothesis, explicitly flagged as one --
and (b) how many lottery draws the fill gets before the ceiling, because each absorbed
reserve-top verdict shuts the window and the next crossing is lent again up to top + 4 KiB,
so each round burns roughly one window.
  - room to burn: shipped G/2 = 16 KiB -> ~4 rounds; R4K 4 KiB + a window -> ~1-2 rounds.
    That matches the NOTE's observed 1-5 refusals per run (mean ~2.9 in the D cell) and
    predicts ~1.3-1.5 under R4K.
  - P(outside per draw) in the machine = 3 bad / (21 sc runs x ~2.5 draws) ~ 6 %.
  - expected bad under R4K = 21 x ~1.3 x 6 % ~ 1.6 of 30, against 3 of 30 today.
So: ABOUT HALF, not the 46 -> 6 (8x) the hermetic three-seed sweep reads, and the second
chance itself is not removed -- the paint still absorbs the first refusal at the same rate,
and the roadmap row's mechanism ("a refusal the program never saw opens the reserve tier")
is still true, just for 4 KiB instead of 16-256 KB.  R4K's own (g) says this in the first
bullet.  Judge its gate's (3) against ~1.6, not against 0: with V at 3 of 30, a one-sided
Fisher at 1 of 30 reads p = 0.30.  THE GATE AS WRITTEN CANNOT REACH SIGNIFICANCE ON (3)
AT 30 RUNS.  R4K half-anticipates this ("(3) is reported, not judged" if V shows < 2 bad),
but the arithmetic needs saying in the other direction too: the capacity and bound claims
((1), (2), (4)) are what this chain can actually establish; (3) will be an observation.
That is not a reason to reject the design.  It is a reason not to let the row be closed on
(3) alone.

1.4  THE FINDING NEITHER R4K NOR THE RECIPIENT PUTS IN ITS HEADLINE: THE LEVER RE-ROLLS
     THE LOTTERY, IT DOES NOT DRAIN IT.
R4K is the only design that ran more than one string-hash seed, and its own pair-seeds.txt
shows the shared rule is NOT MONOTONE:
     seed 1     seed 2     seed 3     total
     21 -> 0     0 -> 6    25 -> 0    46 -> 6
At seed 2 the shipped shim had ZERO second-chance stalls and R4K has SIX.  They are not a
subset of the shipped shim's 46; they are new, at caps (off 9600-9680) where the shipped
shim ended INSIDE the batch's handler.  I re-ran that cap myself (section 6): baseline
term 1 (inside, count 5049), R4K term 2 (the dispatcher: a stall, count 4850), both with
exactly 2 refusals and an identical first refusal.  The second refusal moved from the
batch to the dispatcher because the top moved.
Consequences for practice:
  - The honest claim is "re-rolled, net ~8x better over three seeds hermetically", not
    "removed".  R4K's own (d.2) text says "the lottery is not abolished"; the headline and
    (f)(3) should carry the per-seed split, because a gate that counts only improvements
    will read a worse seed as a design failure, and a row closed on seed 1 will be reopened.
  - THE RECIPIENT's headline "21 outside landings -> 0" is SEED 1 ONLY, and it inherits
    R4K's seed-2 result unchanged, because the rule is the same.  That is the single most
    important thing the RECIPIENT document does not tell its reader.
  - Any in-machine gate must count landings in BOTH directions and must not be scored on a
    single probe phase.  R4K's (f)(3) is a <= comparison against V in the same chain, which
    is the right shape; identify's (2) demands an absolute 0 in 60 runs, which is the wrong
    shape for a re-rolled lottery (and for a probe where STOCK ITSELF stalled 2 of 20 at
    batch 10: results-wall-window, "Stock stalls too").

1.5  THE ADDRESS IN THE MACHINE: THE ONE DESIGN THAT TARGETS THE NOTE'S ABSORBER, AND THE
     TWO WAYS IT CAN FAIL THERE.
What is refused instead of the paint's closure?  Nothing, for as long as the fill keeps
allocating.  The mechanism lines up with the NOTE exactly, and I checked the frame geometry
in the kernel source rather than taking it on faith:
  the sandbox-thread crossing that gets absorbed today is wrapRecursively's FNEW, reached
  as paint -> gpu.set (proxy __index) -> libcomponent.invoke -> invoke -> processResult ->
  wrapUserdata.  The direct path (machine.lua:1126-1148) contains NO pcall, so the nearest
  FRAME_PCALL walking out is the probe's own pcall(paint), and its callee is `paint`.
  checkArg's `check` closure resolves the same way.
So under THE ADDRESS those crossings are read as another handler's, HELD (lent on with the
verdict standing, within the window and 8 KiB more in the burst tier), and the verdict waits
for the fill's next record.  If the fill is the handler of record, the in-machine second
chance goes to ~0 and the 3-of-21 goes with it.  Hermetically that is exactly what happened
in all eight cells: second chances 441/161/166/36/228/72/229/38 -> 0, every refusal caught
by the batch, outside landings 21 -> 0 and 828 -> 776, and no cell worse.  No other design
is monotone across all eight.

FAILURE MODE A: the fill does not win the vote.  The design states this as its limit and
estimates fill ~100, paint ~45, step ~10, dispatcher ~5 allocations per step.  My own count
off the kernel and probe source is less comfortable: at batch 10 in the RECORD shape, the
fill's step is ~8 allocations per object (tostring + rep + concat for uniq(12), two tables,
three for the junk string) = ~80; the paint is 5 gpu.set, each through invoke ->
table.pack(args) -> unwrapUserdata (a `processed` table + its closure) -> target.invoke ->
table.pack(result) -> wrapUserdata (a table + a closure) -> processResult -> table.unpack,
~8-12 allocations each, plus 2-6 concats per message = ~50-70.  So ~80 against ~50-70:
a plurality, not a landslide.  Two mitigations I credit the design for without it saying
so: the vote counts only growths PAST THE CAP (so the ratio near the wall is the ratio that
matters), and gc_handn resets only at a proof that finds the heap under the cap, which near
the wall almost never happens -- so a persistent 55:45 split accumulates a lead of ~10 per
step and the candidate is secure after a few steps.  The hazard is not the margin, it is a
program where the split runs the other way: a quiet producer behind a chatty consumer.
Then THE ADDRESS delivers as today (no regression, no gain) and 94 lines bought nothing.
FAILURE MODE B: the hold overruns its room.  In the BURST tier a non-addressee has
L + H = 12 KiB, which comfortably covers the design's ~4 KB in-machine between-step
estimate.  In the RESERVE tier it has THE WINDOW ONLY, 4 KiB.  A real OpenOS program
reaches the reserve tier whenever it catches one refusal and keeps its data, and OpenOS's
between-resume code is bigger than the hermetic probe's 0.5 KB: event.lua:54-74 packs the
signal (table.pack of event_data) AND COPIES THE WHOLE HANDLER TABLE (`local copy = {}`
plus a slot per handler) on every pullSignal, before pcall(handler.callback); tty and io
buffering sit on top.  If that exceeds 4 KiB while a verdict stands, the hold fails at its
ceiling and the refusal lands in the dispatcher -- the down site.  This is not speculation:
it is revision 1's measured result (probe2k string 828 -> 950, record 162 -> 195, i.e.
+122 and +33 downs) and revision 2 still read 881.  Revision 3 fixed it hermetically
(776, 162) with the standing verdict, but the fix is tuned to a 2 KiB-per-resume model of
a machine whose real figure nobody has measured.  The design is honest about this -- "the
in-machine between-step footprint is the one number this chain still rests on an estimate
for" -- and its gate fails itself on a single bad run with holdfail > 0, which is the right
pre-registration.  It is still the reason not to ship it this week.

1.6  "WHEN A REAL PROGRAM HAS NO HANDLER OF RECORD".
The player's actual OOM is one runaway loop: `for i=1,1e9 do t[i]={} end` at the shell.
Under OpenOS that runs under process.lua:59-72's xpcall of one anonymous function, and it
out-allocates every other handler by orders of magnitude -- THE ADDRESS wins that case
easily, which is worth saying because it is the common case.  The spread case is a program
whose growth is in several of its own callbacks (the dispatcher's pcall(handler.callback)
gives one proto per handler, and the shell has several: the key handler, the timers, the
gpu handler).  Then the Boyer-Moore candidate thrashes, no handler holds the count, and the
verdict is served by sequence -- today's behaviour.  Graceful.  The genuinely awkward case
is the one the design names as W24: a program whose growth MOVES from one of its own pcalls
to another, which is refused up to 12 KiB late, with its recovery intact.  Bounded and
stated.  I find no case where THE ADDRESS is worse than today EXCEPT failure mode B, and
B is a real stall-to-down conversion, which is why it dominates the score.
One instrument hazard in its gate, from this lens: under THE ADDRESS a "second chance" is
redefined from the control's "refusals >= 2 with pf >= 1" to "holds >= 1 && refusals == 1".
The Fisher test in (b) then compares two NON-IDENTICAL events across builds.  The design
states the substitution; it should also state that (b) is therefore a comparison of rates
of different observables, and keep the control's own definition as a second, reported count.

4.2  THE BEST SINGLE TEST OF THE ROUND IS R4K's W21, AND IT IS THE ONE THE RECIPIENT LACKS.
R4K reports a surprising reading against its own interest: W8 PASSES WITH THE RESERVE SET
TO ZERO (R0: 80/81, W19 only).  W8 runs on the main thread -- the kernel, with its 16 KiB
slice -- and its one allocation while holding is covered by THE WINDOW's lend.  So the case
the map's section 7 names as the reserve's reason does not in fact guard the reserve's
size.  R4K then BUILT the case that does: W21, "a recovery that holds across the cycles its
report costs" (as the sandbox, ~2 KB kept, ~8 KB granted, the armed cycles in between),
which FAILS on R0 and R1K and passes on shipped/R4K/R8K.  That is how you find the FLOOR
of a constant, and it is why I believe 4 KiB rather than taking it on assertion.  It also
found a floor nobody chose: with the JIT on, 1 KiB fails W16Rj/W16RjL because the recorder's
own allocations share the reserve.
THE RECIPIENT has no equivalent.  Its R4 bounds the reserve from ABOVE (u2-u1 <= 4 KiB +
window + 1 KiB) and from below only by 512 B of growth.  Nothing in its 85 checks says
4 KiB is ENOUGH for a recovery; its only size sabotage is reserveG (too big).  Its test set
is one-directional on the number it chose.  For a maintainer that is the difference between
a constant with a measured floor and a constant with a plausible value.
THE ADDRESS's new cases are the most elaborate (W20-W25, two handlers on one thread, a vote
overtake, a standing verdict surviving a proof) and its sabotage matrix is the tightest
(each fails exactly its set, logs in sab6/) -- except rawverdict (4.1 above).  Its W24 is a
guard, not fail-first, and it exists because revision 1 killed that sandbox: worth grafting
(section 5).

4.3  THE ONE BEHAVIOUR THE RECIPIENT LOSES THAT R4K KEEPS.
For a refusal taken BELOW the burst top, the RECIPIENT's clamp floor gives exactly G/2 (the
burst top) while R4K gives G/2 + 4 KiB.  Its own (g) reports the consequence: the shipped
shim's reserve let a 32 KiB holder doubling at cap + 2 KiB succeed on retry (ctl_rec.lua
"does not reproduce under rule 3"), and in the machine's JIT-on string cells at 192 KB that
is the 16 KiB doubling at 2 048 objects -- 5 of 15 W/V runs got it through and held 2 049;
they will hold 2 048.  The object count is trivial; the SHAPE is not, because "grow a table
just past the cap, catch, retry" is ordinary Lua.  Checking the arithmetic at the floor:
used = cap + 2 KiB, G = 32 KiB.  R4K's top = cap + max(2K, 16K) + 4K = cap + 20 K, so
18 K fits: GRANTED.  RECIPIENT's top = cap + clamp(6K, 16K, 32K) = cap + 16 K: REFUSED.
Stock refuses it too, so neither is a contract break -- but R4K is the friendlier of two
rules that cost the same elsewhere, and it gets there with fewer lines.

5. THE PROBES AND THE GATES: CAN EACH FAIL?  IS THE CONTROL ARM SOUND?  WHAT IS MEASURED?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  CAN IT FAIL?
  R4K (f): BEST OF THE THREE.  Every criterion names the control that shows it can fail;
    (1) voids the whole chain unless V's own od_peak check fails in >= 10 of its 30 JIT-on
    runs; (2) voids a cell where the probe took no second chance; (3) is judged only if V
    shows >= 2 bad, with a stated reps top-up and an explicit "reported, not judged"
    fallback; (7) PRE-COMMITS TO THE NEGATIVE RESULT -- if the extra refusals turn out to be
    S1 or the OpenOS error path, "this design does not address them and the row stays open
    with that finding".  That is the j2 lesson absorbed.  Residual: (3) cannot reach
    significance at 30 runs (section 1.3), and the criterion should say so.
  ADDRESS (f): second best and the most self-aware.  (a) teeth >= 8 of 15 with VOID below
    6; (b) a Fisher bar PLUS "a down with holdfail > 0 FAILS the gate" -- a clause aimed at
    its own known hazard, which is exactly how to pre-register; and it says outright that
    the main session's instrumented run "can settle first, and should" the per-handler
    allocation count, and that if the paint out-allocates the fill, "(b) will not be met;
    that is the design's stated limit, not a defect to be hidden by the gate".  Residual:
    the redefined second-chance observable (1.5).
  RECIPIENT (f): weakest.  (1) teeth are there (>= 20 of 60 or VOID).  But (2) asks for an
    absolute 0 DOWN+STALL+PROC-DEATH in 60 runs on a probe where STOCK stalled 2 of 20 at
    batch 10 and where the lever is known (1.4) to re-roll in both directions; and its own
    stated significance is p = 0.035, against the p < 0.01 bar this project's last two gates
    used, with no note that the bar moved.  Its control arm is 60 candidate runs against 20
    V runs, unbalanced, plus a POOLED W+V history (21 of 30, 3 down) -- and the results doc
    warns W and V are different builds.  R4K and THE ADDRESS both interleave by rep against
    a same-chain V; that is the sound arm and the RECIPIENT should adopt it.

5.2  DOES IT MEASURE THE MACHINE OR THE PROBE?  (applies to all three)
  - probe2oe is the j2 JUDGE's MODEL of OpenOS's event.timer (three checkArg closures), not
    OpenOS; probe2k_2048 is a model of Java's signal pushes.  The forensics say so
    (section 8: "models of the machine, not the machine").  Every "21 -> 0" and "46 -> 6"
    in this round is measured on those models.
  - the in-machine absorber is structurally DIFFERENT from the hermetic one (1.1): kernel
    closures inside a component call, not the probe's own string.format.
  - the probe itself perturbs what it measures: OCLJCAPX's row-19 paint adds ~1.1 KB of live
    data and a FIFTH gpu.set per paint, and the probe's own comment says "compare a run only
