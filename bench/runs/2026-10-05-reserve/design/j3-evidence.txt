1. THE W8 QUESTION, SETTLED (asked explicitly; it bears on all three)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
W8 (mem_test.c:1866-1896, chunk :555-562) runs `runstr(W, W8_CHUNK)` on the MAIN state.
There is no `_OCLJ_WATCHDOG.arm` anywhere in the case, so wd_depth is 0, and
lj52_gc_kernel (lj52shim.c:1190-1193) is `M->wd_depth == 0 || ...` -- it reads TRUE.
Consequences, by code:
  - lj52_gc_credit (:1197) gives W8's thread G/2 + LJ52_GC_KSLICE (16 KiB) in BURST;
  - lj52_gc_lend (:1229) returns 0 for the kernel: W8 NEVER GETS A WINDOW LEND;
  - lj52_gc_refused (:1251) has no kernel exclusion, so W8's refusal does open RESERVE.
So the room that pays for W8's 'err: ' .. tostring(err) while the data is held is the
KERNEL'S SLICE, not the window and not the reserve.
MEASURED, decisively: W8's final `used` is 1 142 418 B on the shipped shim and on R0, R1K,
R4K, R8K and L4 ALIKE (lifetime/mtout/{base-lt4,R0-lt,R1K-lt,R4K-lt4,R8K-lt,L4-lt}.out,
all with cap 1 091 346).  W8 is insensitive to the reserve's size and lifetime to the byte.
  -> lifetime's (c) states the right conclusion ("W8 does not guard the reserve's size;
     W21 does") but the wrong mechanism ("covered by THE WINDOW's lend").  Correct the
     sentence to the kernel's slice.  The R0 datum it rests on is sound.
  -> identify's (c) argues W8 passes because "W8's refusal is the program's own ... it
     opens the reserve with credit max(G/2, r + 4 KiB - total)".  True and measured, but
     vacuous as evidence for the risk identify creates: W8's recovery is paid from KSLICE.
     identify has NO sandbox-side recovery case.  (Its (g) does name the hazard -- "a
     traceback of more than ~4 KiB after a second refusal may not" fit -- and leaves it
     untested.)
  -> verdict's (c) says "the fill is the only handler, so it is the handler of record".
     Under lj52_gc_vote the kernel branch sets gc_addr = 1 and returns before any frame
     walk, so W8 exercises neither the vote nor the hold: it is vacuous for THE ADDRESS.
     verdict is nonetheless covered here, because its W20-W25 run as the sandbox (a
     coroutine under the arm) and each ends with tail(): format while held, drop,
     string.rep(256), {1,2,3}, asserted as 'err: not enough memory|256|3'.
  -> FOR THE ROUND: the hard constraint "W8 keeps passing" is met by all three and
     DISCRIMINATES NONE.  The constraint should be re-stated as "a SANDBOX program's
     catch-format-drop survives, with a proof between catch and drop".  Only lifetime's
     W21 (fails on R0 and R1K: result -2) and verdict's W20/W24 tails test that.
     verdict's W8 line is also the measurement of its kernel cost: used 1 142 386
     (shipped, its own test file) -> 1 150 570 (prototype) = +8 184 B = exactly
     LJ52_GC_HOLD.  The kernel-thread excursion grows by H in the burst tier.
