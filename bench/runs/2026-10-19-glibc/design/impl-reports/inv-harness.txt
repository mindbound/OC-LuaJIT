SUMMARY: The harness runs on Linux. A scratch copy of smoke-test.sh with three small edits ran in AlmaLinux 8 (glibc 2.28, JDK 1.8.0_504, the same JDK update the Windows chain uses) against the trial .so, the one with six unversioned pthread UND symbols. The JVM loaded it, and the additive JIT-on arm scored CHECKS 82, FAILURES 0, VERDICT PASS [M]. Its 82 milestone ids and verdicts are identical to Windows r3-additive-on-f [M]. That includes k1 and k5 (the watchdog thread fired) and gc-1..3 and vd-1..9 (the Value finalizer's upcall into Java ran). The stock arm scored 53/0, with its 60 milestone lines identical to Windows r3-stock-f [M]. These are the first JVM runs of a Linux native (roadmap row 216). The six symbols bind to /lib64/libpthread.so.0 on Alma and to libc.so.6 on Ubuntu, and only because the JVM already has those libraries loaded (LD_DEBUG=bindings with LD_BIND_NOW=1) [M]. One arm takes about 85-94 s of wall time on /mnt/c, where Windows took about 100 s [M]. All seven arms of chain-r3 can run on Linux after the edits. I wrote a Linux launcher, lx-full.sh, and both arms ran through it. A side finding, outside my role but release-relevant: Alma has no git, so build-native.sh bakes 'unknown' where Windows has '1ee778a4' into the .so's blob fingerprint. eris refuses a blob whose fingerprint differs, so Windows-written blobs would not restore on the Alma-built .so [M][S].
